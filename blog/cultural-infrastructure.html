Opera as Cultural Infrastructure: Filling the Gap in Jerusalem

May 28, 2025 Opera Shalem · 4 min read

How permanent arts institutions strengthen communities and economies.

The Cultural Landscape of Jerusalem

Jerusalem stands among the world's most historically and spiritually significant cities—a living museum of human civilization spanning thousands of years. Visitors flock to its ancient walls to witness the convergence of three major world religions and to experience its rich tapestry of cultural influences.

Yet for all its historical and religious significance, Jerusalem has lacked a crucial element that defines great global cities: a thriving, permanent opera house that serves as both a cultural landmark and a center for artistic expression. This absence represents not merely a gap in entertainment options but a fundamental missing piece in Jerusalem's cultural identity.

The Historical Context of Opera in Israel

Israel's relationship with opera has been complex and evolving. Opera in Israel has a rich history dating back to 1947, when American soprano Edis De Philippe established the Israel National Opera. This company operated from 1947 to 1982, performing over 1,000 productions, but its operations were primarily centered in Tel Aviv.

Following De Philippe's death, the Israel National Opera eventually closed in 1982. It was later resuscitated as the New Israeli Opera in Tel Aviv in 1985, which has since served as the nation's primary operatic institution. However, this renewed operatic presence has remained concentrated in Tel Aviv's cultural scene, leaving Jerusalem—despite being Israel's capital—without regular access to this art form.

Prior attempts to establish opera in Jerusalem have been sporadic and insufficient. The Jerusalem Opera, founded in 2011, has offered occasional productions but lacks a permanent venue and institutional support to provide a consistent presence in the city's cultural calendar.

The void is particularly notable given Jerusalem's multicultural character. Opera, with its ability to transcend language barriers through music and universal themes, offers a unique opportunity to bridge the city's diverse communities.

Why Jerusalem Needs Permanent Opera Infrastructure

A Vehicle for Cultural Expression

In a city where divisions often dominate headlines, the establishment of Opera Shalem represents an opportunity for cultural expression that few other initiatives can match. Opera, by its nature, draws from diverse traditions and speaks to universal human experiences—love, loss, redemption, and the struggle against adversity.

The Israel Philharmonic Orchestra's 2013 performance of Verdi's Requiem at the Sultan's Pool in Jerusalem demonstrated the power of classical music to bring together diverse audiences. The outdoor concert attracted over 8,000 attendees from all sectors of Jerusalem society, proving that there exists a hunger for high cultural experiences.

Economic Development and Cultural Tourism

Beyond its artistic and social value, Opera Shalem represents a significant economic opportunity for Jerusalem. Cities worldwide have demonstrated how cultural institutions can drive economic development, tourism, and urban revitalization.

Cultural tourists typically stay longer and spend significantly more per day than standard tourists. They are also more likely to engage with local businesses beyond the main tourist attractions. Opera audiences, in particular, represent a demographic with higher discretionary spending capacity, creating economic ripple effects throughout the local economy.

Jerusalem's tourism industry has traditionally focused on religious and historical attractions. Adding a world-class opera venue would diversify this offering, potentially extending tourist stays and attracting new demographic segments interested in cultural tourism.

Nurturing Local Talent

Perhaps most significantly for Jerusalem's long-term cultural development, Opera Shalem would provide a crucial platform for nurturing local talent. Currently, Jerusalem's promising young musicians, composers, and performers often leave the city—and sometimes Israel entirely—to pursue careers where institutional support for classical music is stronger.

The Jerusalem Academy of Music and Dance produces excellent instrumentalists and performers across various disciplines, yet the absence of professional opera in the city means these graduates lack local opportunities for career development.

The creation of a professional opera company requires not just vocal performers, but also orchestral musicians, designers, directors, technicians, administrators, and creative professionals—creating a comprehensive ecosystem that can sustain a diverse artistic community.

The Vision for Opera Shalem

Architectural and Cultural Integration

The proposed permanent Opera Shalem facility represents our long-term vision. The facility would not be merely transplanted from European traditions but would be designed to reflect Jerusalem's unique character and history, envisioning a net-zero opera house that utilizes sustainable energy while harmonizing with Jerusalem's iconic skyline.

Programming that Reflects Diversity

Opera Shalem's artistic vision extends beyond the standard repertoire to include works that reflect the diverse cultural traditions of Jerusalem and the broader Middle East. This approach would include commissioning new operas based on Middle Eastern stories and themes, incorporating musical elements from multiple traditions, presenting works in Hebrew and other relevant languages, and developing educational programs that engage Jerusalem's diverse communities.

The Israeli Opera's recent success with their commissioned work Theodor, an opera about Theodor Herzl, demonstrates the powerful resonance of locally-relevant operatic productions. Opera Shalem aims to build on this precedent, creating works that speak directly to Jerusalem's unique cultural context.

From Vision to Reality

The transformation of Opera Shalem from vision to reality will require collective effort from multiple stakeholders: private philanthropy from both Israeli and international donors, community engagement to ensure the institution serves all Jerusalemites, partnerships with existing cultural and educational institutions, and international collaboration with established opera companies.

Cultural Completeness

Jerusalem's identity as a holy city need not preclude its development as a cultural capital. Indeed, throughout history, spiritual centers have often been hubs of artistic innovation and excellence. From the Psalms of David to the intricate mosaics of ancient synagogues and churches, Jerusalem's religious heritage has always been intertwined with artistic expression.

Opera Shalem represents not a departure from Jerusalem's character but an evolution consistent with its historical role as a center of human creativity and expression. By establishing a world-class opera institution in Jerusalem, we fulfill an artistic birthright and create a legacy that will enrich generations to come.
