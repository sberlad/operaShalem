When Lions Rise: Jewish Culture in a Time of Conflict

June 17, 2025 Opera Shalem · 3 min read

Why cultural expression matters most when the world wants us silent.

As Israel launched Operation Rising Lion in response to decades of Iranian threats, we were reminded that defense isn't just about military strength—it's about cultural survival. For years, Iran and its proxies have targeted Jews worldwide, seeking to silence not just our voices, but our stories, our art, our very identity.

The War on Jewish Expression

The attacks haven't been limited to physical violence. A more insidious strategy has emerged: cultural erasure. Across the globe, Jewish artists face systematic exclusion—passed over for roles, denied opportunities, forced to hide their identities to pursue their craft.

But the erasure goes deeper than excluding Jewish talent. It's about controlling the narrative itself. Turn on any screen today and the pattern becomes clear: Jews appear as victims only in Holocaust stories, or as morally compromised Israeli operatives. When we're not villains, we're reduced to cultural caricatures that strip away authentic identity while claiming representation.

The irony is stark: despite the significant Jewish presence in the entertainment industry, there are remarkably few truly Jewish characters or authentically Jewish stories. Where are the complex, proud, unapologetic Jewish characters? Where are the stories that celebrate rather than diminish us?

This isn't accidental. It's the logical endpoint of making Jewish identity itself a liability in the arts world.

Culture as Resistance

When we founded Opera Shalem, we understood something profound: creating authentic Jewish art isn't just cultural work. It's an act of resistance. Every Jewish story told with pride, every Hebrew melody that rises from Jerusalem's ancient stones, every artist who chooses homeland over exile is a declaration that we will not be silenced.

The Lion of Judah doesn't just defend territory. It defends identity.

The Symbol of Opera Shalem

Our Opera Shalem logo captures this perfectly: the Lion of Judah from Jerusalem's crest, holding a lyre, his head raised in song. We chose this ancient symbol of our holy city because it represents everything we stand for.

This isn't just artistic symbolism—it's a declaration. Where others see the lion only as a warrior, we see the complete picture: strength and beauty, power and creativity, defense and expression united in one magnificent creature. The lion that defends also creates. The lion that roars also sings.

This image embodies everything Opera Shalem represents: Jerusalem's own lion bringing together Jewish strength with Jewish artistry, the understanding that cultural creation is itself an act of resistance, and the vision that even in times of conflict, the lion's song must never be silenced.

A Light Beyond Borders

But our vision extends beyond Jewish pride alone. Opera Shalem aspires to become a cultural beacon that demonstrates how theater can be economically and ecologically sustainable, built on kindness rather than exploitation, innovative in approach, and genuinely growth-oriented for every person involved.

Despite all the adversity we face, we choose to inspire not just the Jewish people but the entire world. This is perhaps the most Jewish response of all: to transform suffering into beauty, persecution into purpose, and isolation into illumination.

Why Jerusalem, Why Now

In this climate, Israel must become more than a refuge—it must be a place where Jewish culture thrives without compromise. Opera Shalem represents this vision: a fortress for Jewish expression and a model for how creativity and humanity can flourish together.

There's something symbolic worth noting: the lion was once the proud symbol of pre-revolutionary Iran, representing an ancient, noble civilization before extremist ideology consumed it. Perhaps we're witnessing not just the Lion of Judah defending our people, but the possibility of cultural nobility rising again.

We're not just building an opera house. We're building a model for how creativity and humanity can flourish together, even under pressure.

The Sound of Defiance

When Opera Shalem's curtain rises, it will carry a message that echoes far beyond Jerusalem: Jewish culture will not be diminished, Jewish voices will not be silenced, and Jewish stories will be told with the full power and beauty they deserve.

But it will also demonstrate something else: that even in the darkest times, we choose to create light for others to follow.

This is what it means to rise like lions—not just in defense, but in creation, celebration, and the unashamed belief that our best response to hatred is to make the world more beautiful.
