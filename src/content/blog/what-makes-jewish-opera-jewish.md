<h2>The Question That Defines Our Work</h2>

<p>When people hear that Opera Shalem aims to create a distinctly Jewish operatic tradition, the first assumption is usually about content: biblical stories, Jewish historical figures, Hebrew-language libretti. But creating authentically Jewish opera goes far deeper than surface-level cultural markers.</p>

<p>The real question isn't <em>what</em> Jewish opera is about. It's <em>how</em> Jewish sensibility, values, and artistic traditions shape the very structure and sound of operatic expression.</p>

<h2>What National Operatic Traditions Teach Us</h2>

<p>Every culture with a strong operatic tradition has used it to express cultural identity. <a href="https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giuseppe-Verdi" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Italian opera</a> emerged from Italian literary and musical traditions, shaping how melody and drama interact. <a href="https://www.biography.com/musicians/richard-wagner" target="_blank" rel="noopener">German opera</a> grappled with German mythology and philosophy, creating different structural principles. Russian opera told Russian stories in distinctly Russian musical language.</p>

<p>These weren't simply operas <em>about</em> Italy or Germany or Russia. They were operas that sounded Italian, German, or Russian because they emerged from those cultures' musical DNA.</p>

<h3>The Jewish Musical Tradition</h3>

<p>Jewish musical tradition is extraordinarily rich: <a href="https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/cantorial-music/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">cantorial singing</a>, Klezmer instrumental traditions, Sephardic piyyutim, Yemenite chanting, liturgical modes and scales. Each carries centuries of accumulated artistic wisdom.</p>

<p>What does opera sound like when it emerges from <a href="https://www.milkenarchive.org/radio/category/radio-programs/episode-8-cantorial-tradition/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">cantorial vocal traditions</a> rather than Italian bel canto? How does the Jewish calendar—with its rhythms of Shabbat, holidays, and seasons—shape institutional structure differently than Christian liturgical seasons shaped European opera houses?</p>

<h2>Jewish Values as Artistic Principles</h2>

<p>Perhaps more importantly, Jewish opera emerges from Jewish values and ways of engaging with the world.</p>

<p><strong>Argumentation and interpretation:</strong> Jewish textual tradition values debate, multiple interpretations, questioning. What happens when that principle shapes dramatic structure? Opera that embraces ambiguity rather than resolution, that presents questions rather than answers?</p>

<p><strong>Collective responsibility:</strong> Unlike tragic heroes isolated by fate, Jewish tradition emphasizes communal obligation. How does this reshape operatic protagonists and their relationships to chorus, to community?</p>

<p><strong>Memory and witness:</strong> Jewish culture carries an imperative to remember, to bear witness. This creates different narrative relationships to history than other operatic traditions.</p>

<h2>Language as Cultural Expression</h2>

<p>Language matters, but not in the obvious way. Jewish opera doesn't require Hebrew any more than American opera requires English. <a href="https://www.operabase.com/statistics/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Most opera worldwide</a> is performed in the original language with supertitles.</p>

<p>But language carries cultural weight. Hebrew brings thousands of years of textual resonance. Yiddish carries particular emotional and cultural associations. Ladino connects to Sephardic tradition. Multilingual opera reflects diasporic experience.</p>

<p>The question isn't which language to use but how language choice serves artistic and cultural purposes.</p>

<h2>Contemporary Jewish Experience</h2>

<p>Jewish opera in 2026 must also engage with contemporary Jewish reality. What does it mean to create Jewish art in a moment when <a href="https://www.ajc.org/news/the-state-of-antisemitism-in-america-2024-AJC-survey" target="_blank" rel="noopener">antisemitism is rising globally</a>, when Jewish artists face systematic exclusion from cultural institutions, when Israel fights for survival against Iranian proxies?</p>

<p>In February 2026, <a href="https://www.britannica.com/event/2026-Iran-Conflict" target="_blank" rel="noopener">the United States and Israel killed Iranian Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei</a>, triggering full-scale war. Days later, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Hezbollah%E2%80%93Israel_strikes" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Hezbollah broke its ceasefire with Israel</a>, opening a second front. As we write this in March 2026, Israel fights for its existence.</p>

<p>Jewish opera cannot pretend these realities don't exist. But neither should it reduce Jewish identity to victimhood or conflict. The challenge is creating work that acknowledges contemporary struggle while asserting the fullness of Jewish cultural expression.</p>

<h2>Opera Shalem's Approach</h2>

<p>At <a href="/#about">Opera Shalem</a>, we're committed to developing Jewish operatic voice through multiple approaches:</p>

<p><strong>Commissioning new works</strong> by composers and librettists who bring Jewish sensibility to their craft, whether or not the works have explicitly Jewish content.</p>

<p><strong>Reimagining canonical opera</strong> through Jewish interpretive traditions. What happens when we bring midrashic reading practices to Mozart? When we approach Verdi with Talmudic argumentation methods?</p>

<p><strong>Engaging with Jewish musical traditions</strong> not through superficial quotation but through deep structural influence. How do cantorial modal systems reshape harmonic language? How does Klezmer ornamentation inform vocal writing?</p>

<p><strong>Building <a href="/#model">institutional structures</a></strong> that reflect Jewish values: Shabbat observance affecting rehearsal schedules, collaborative rather than hierarchical artistic processes, commitment to economic sustainability for artists.</p>

<h2>No Single Answer</h2>

<p>There is no single definition of Jewish opera, just as there's no single way to be Jewish. Ashkenazi, Sephardi, Mizrahi, Ethiopian—each tradition brings different musical languages. Secular, Reform, Conservative, Orthodox—each brings different values and sensibilities.</p>

<p>What makes opera Jewish isn't adherence to prescribed forms but emergence from Jewish experience and engagement with Jewish artistic traditions. It's work that couldn't have been created by anyone else, because it carries the specific cultural DNA of Jewish creativity.</p>

<p>As we develop Opera Shalem's repertoire and artistic identity, we're guided not by rigid definitions but by serious engagement with the question itself. What stories do we need to tell? What sounds carry our cultural memory? What artistic traditions can we draw from and contribute to?</p>

<p>Jewish opera, like <a href="/#tech">Jewish culture</a> itself, is something we create through the doing. And in Jerusalem—the city that has inspired Jewish creativity for millennia—we're building the institutional home where that creation can flourish for generations to come.</p>
