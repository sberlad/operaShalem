<h2>Thank You for Responding</h2>

<p>When we sent our first newsletter—opening with the question 'What if the ancient stones of Jerusalem could sing?'—we didn't know what to expect. Would anyone care about building an opera company in a city that's never had one?</p>

<p>The answer came quickly: <strong>Yes.</strong></p>

<p>Dozens of responses arrived within days. Opera singers writing from Vienna, Tel Aviv, and New York. Philanthropists asking how they could help. Israeli artists expressing relief that someone was finally building permanent cultural infrastructure. Diaspora Jews sharing what Jerusalem means to them.</p>

<h2>What We Heard</h2>

<h3>From Artists</h3>

<p>Professional singers told us about the impossible choice they face: pursue a career in opera, which means living abroad, or make aliyah and abandon their art form. Several mentioned colleagues who'd moved to Israel only to return to Europe within a year because no sustainable work existed.</p>

<p>One bass-baritone wrote: <em>'I've been considering aliyah for years, but as a Wagner singer, where would I work? This could change everything.'</em></p>

<h3>From Donors and Partners</h3>

<p>Many asked practical questions: What's the timeline? What's the capital requirement? How does <a href="/#house">an ensemble model</a> differ from project-based opera?</p>

<p>But more importantly, several expressed something deeper: a sense that Jerusalem <em>should</em> have an opera house. That cultural completeness (shalem) matters. That a capital city without permanent cultural institutions feels incomplete.</p>

<h3>From Jerusalemites</h3>

<p>Residents of Jerusalem wrote with both excitement and skepticism. Excitement that someone was thinking seriously about cultural infrastructure. Skepticism that anything could actually get built in a city notorious for bureaucratic complexity.</p>

<p>These responses were valuable. They reminded us that Opera Shalem must earn its place in the city—not through grandiose claims, but through thoughtful development, community engagement, and demonstrated commitment to <a href="/#design">sustainability</a>.</p>

<h2>How Your Feedback Is Shaping Our Work</h2>

<h3>We're Starting With Artists</h3>

<p>Many respondents emphasized that infrastructure doesn't mean just buildings—it means <em>people</em>. So we're beginning by building relationships with singers, conductors, directors, and designers who share our vision.</p>

<p>The ensemble model means we're not just hiring artists for individual productions. We're creating long-term employment that allows artists to grow, develop, and build institutional memory together.</p>

<h3>We're Being Transparent About Timeline and Capital</h3>

<p>Several responses asked for clarity about our development path. Here it is:</p>

<p><strong>Phase 1 (Current):</strong> $250k seed phase for legal incorporation, <a href="/#company">core team</a> formation, and capital campaign launch.</p>

<p><strong>Phase 2:</strong> Artistic planning, partnership development, site selection, and architectural feasibility studies.</p>

<p><strong>Phase 3:</strong> $250M capital campaign for permanent facility construction.</p>

<p><strong>Phase 4:</strong> Ensemble formation, repertoire development, and inaugural season.</p>

<p>This is a multi-year process. We're not promising opening night next season. We're building something designed to last generations.</p>

<h3>We're Emphasizing Cultural Infrastructure</h3>

<p>Many respondents resonated with the phrase 'cultural infrastructure.' It captures something important: this isn't a startup or a project. It's permanent <a href="/#company">institutional development</a>.</p>

<p>We're studying successful models—European opera houses with centuries of history, American companies with strong governance, Israeli cultural institutions that have achieved sustainability—and adapting what works for Jerusalem's unique context.</p>

<h2>What Comes Next</h2>

<p>We're expanding our network of partners, patrons, and supporters. If you're reading this and haven't yet joined our mailing list, <a href="/#partner">subscribe here</a>.</p>

<p>We're preparing detailed feasibility studies on repertoire, ensemble size, facility requirements, and economic impact.</p>

<p>And we're continuing to have conversations—with artists, donors, cultural leaders, and anyone who believes Jerusalem deserves complete cultural infrastructure.</p>

<p>Your early support is what makes this possible. This isn't about one person's vision. It's about building something together—something that will outlast all of us.</p>

<p>Thank you for being part of it.</p>
